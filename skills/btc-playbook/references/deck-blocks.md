# Deck building blocks

Pick the blocks the brief demands, add the ones the argument needs, drop the rest.
Each block below lists what "good" requires. Use these as acceptance criteria in deck-qa.

## Problem framing
Use 2–3 dimensions: the **mechanism** (why it happens, not the symptom), why it matters **now**,
who it hurts and **how much**, second-order effects, the regulatory angle only where load-bearing.

## Company positioning / right-to-win
Most important slide in market-entry cases and the most skipped. Name the specific assets that
give THIS company an advantage a new entrant lacks. "Wide customer reach" is not a right to win;
"purchase-intent data plus an existing payments relationship with an overlapping base" is closer.
In multi-sided markets, state which side you solve for, which you constrain, and what you are
NOT doing.

## Personas (2–3 max)
For each: context and **behaviour** (not demographics); needs/jobs framed independently of your
solution; current pain **and current workaround**. The workaround matters more than the pain: no
workaround may mean no real pain. State **why this persona was prioritised**, with the criterion
visibly applied.

## User journey / flow
Earns its slide only if it reveals **where the problem lives**. Stages across; rows for actions,
touchpoints, emotion, pain points, and **the metric that would detect the problem at each stage**.
For system behaviour use a flowchart with decision nodes and failure paths; a happy-path-only
flow describes a demo, not a system.

## MVP
Two different things: **MVP vision** (what this becomes if it works) and **MVP formulation**
(what ships first and what does not). Attach a hypothesis: what you believe, and what result
would falsify it.

## Features / solution
Each feature: what it does, who it is for, why it beats the alternative.

## Prioritisation
Show the **criteria**; let the ranking fall out of them. Apply the framework visibly with actual
scores and where they came from. An unpopulated framework diagram is decoration.

## Metrics (see metrics-stack.md)
North Star + 2–4 inputs + outputs + guardrails + counter-metrics, **with targets**.

## Business model
Revenue streams named and sized; pricing structure with a reason; pricing strategy (cost-plus,
competitive, value-based, penetration, premium); unit economics with cost lines shown. Every
number traces to a stated assumption.

## Roadmap
The axis signals thinking: phases with **gates** beat dates; or by persona/capability/market.

## Go-to-market (five answers)
1. Who first, and why ahead of others. 2. Positioning in one line. 3. Channels (online/offline,
existing/new users) **with acquisition economics**. 4. Price and launch incentives where material.
5. What must be true operationally (supply side too, on marketplaces). Name cities, segments and
partners **in order, with the reason for the order**. Name exact places users are, not generic
channels.

## Risks (3–5)
Each with a mitigation and, ideally, a **leading indicator** that it is materialising. Generic
"execution risk" is filler.

## Novel ideas still need numbers
Estimated economics, rough capital need, path to profitability, which business metric moves.
Benchmarks and guesstimates are fine; nothing is not.
