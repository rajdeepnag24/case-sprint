# AI usage rules (winning teams drew the line here)

**AI fills in structure the human built. It never originates the idea or the narrative.**

## Use AI for
- Deep research; getting up to speed on an unfamiliar industry fast.
- Filling sections once storyline and headline messages are set.
- Curating and tightening content; refining argument sequence once the argument is known.
- Stress-testing: gaps, weak assumptions, loopholes before an evaluator finds them.
- Generating likely panel questions to prepare answers.

## Never use AI for
- Feeding the problem statement and asking for ideas to pick from. This produces the submission
  that looks like everyone else's; evaluators read in batches and have flagged generic AI ideas.
- Choosing the angle, the storyline flow, or the message to land.

## How case-sprint enforces this
- Gate 2 (angle + hypothesis) and Gate 3 (headlines) are owned by the human.
- When asked to "suggest angles", present **evidence and tensions** found in research, plus at most
  three framings labelled as prompts for thinking; require the human to state the chosen angle in
  their own words before continuing.
- Never converge squad members on a shared solution; structural feedback and tooling are fine.

## AI inside the proposed product (when the case involves AI)
Start from the user problem; ask whether AI changes the solution space. Strong fit: unstructured
input, natural language, generation, multimodal, context-heavy assistance. Usually not AI:
calculations, policy rules, exact validation, transactional state, deterministic workflows.
State the AI job (classify / extract / summarise / generate / recommend / answer / predict / act),
compare against the non-AI option, and scale the technical bar to consequence (grounding,
validation, human review, monitoring, fallback). Mental model: model + context + tools +
guardrails + evals. Think cost per completed task, not per call.
