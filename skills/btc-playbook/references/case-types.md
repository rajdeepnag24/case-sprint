# The six case types

Almost every brief is a variant of one of six shapes. A brief can straddle two: name the
**dominant** type, let the secondary type shape the appendix. If none fits, say so and treat it
as "open problem" (use type 1 or 2 logic, whichever is closer).

| # | Type | What it really tests | Signal words in briefs | Classic failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Improve an existing product** | Depth: specific users and mechanisms, not strategy altitude | "improve", "increase X for product Y", "users complain", "engagement/retention of…" | Proposing a new business when an improvement was asked; staying generic |
| 2 | **Build a new product inside an existing company** | Constraint: defining scope, MVP, success | "design a module/feature/tool for…", "choose one of three modules", "wireframes required" | Over-scoping; MVP that is the whole vision |
| 3 | **Enter a new vertical or market** | Right-to-win, not opportunity size | "enter", "launch in", "expand into", "should X build Y" | Big TAM slide, no reason why THIS company wins vs incumbents |
| 4 | **Make a strategic bet** | Conviction + quality of rejection reasoning | "pick one direction", "choose between", "revenue target by year 3", "explain why rejected" | Hedging: picking a path then building something that works for all paths |
| 5 | **Diagnose and resolve a systemic problem** | Holding competing stakeholder interests | "metric is falling", "merchants/consumers/couriers/regulators", "should we solve this at all" | Collapsing into one side; solving the symptom |
| 6 | **Monetise an asset** | Commercial imagination + market sizing | "data products", "new revenue from…", "what could we sell", "size the market" | Ideas with no buyer, no pricing, no sizing |

## Toolkit per type (modules from `case-modules`)

| Type | Must-run modules | Usually add | Distinctive slide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Improve | users-and-jobs, journey-and-pain, prioritisation, metrics-stack | mvp, risks | Journey map showing *where* the problem lives |
| 2 New product | users-and-jobs, mvp, prioritisation, metrics-stack | economics, gtm, prototype | MVP vision vs MVP formulation + falsifiable hypothesis |
| 3 New vertical | right-to-win, market-sizing, gtm, economics | users-and-jobs, risks | Right-to-win: specific assets an entrant lacks |
| 4 Strategic bet | options-and-rejection, economics, roadmap-gates | metrics-stack, risks | Options compared on stated criteria + why each loser lost |
| 5 Systemic | stakeholder-map, root-cause, precedents, metrics-stack (with guardrails), governance | mvp, roadmap-gates | Multi-sided trade-off: who you solve for, who you constrain |
| 6 Monetise | market-sizing, buyers-and-pricing, gtm, economics | metrics-stack, risks | Product → buyer → price → size chain |

## Ambition rule

Match the ambition to the brief. "Improve the product" → a well-reasoned improvement, not a new
business. "Make a new bet" → incrementalism reads as timidity. The question sets the direction,
not your appetite for novelty.
