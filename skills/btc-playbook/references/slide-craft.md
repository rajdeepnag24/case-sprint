# Slide craft

A case deck is **read, not presented**. It must stand alone.

## Storyline before slides
Write the argument as a sequence of complete assertions, one per intended slide. Test: someone
reading only that sequence still gets your answer.

- Weak (topics): "Problem. Personas. Solution. Metrics. Roadmap." Fits any case; says nothing;
  cannot be wrong.
- Strong (claims): "Menu-price inflation is a trust problem, not a pricing problem, concentrated in
  SMB merchants without pricing tools. It costs the platform more in lost conversion than it saves
  merchants in margin. So give merchants the tooling to price competitively before penalising them.
  Measure on repeat rate, not parity compliance. Pilot in two SMB-heavy cities with a gate before
  scale." Same five slides, but each line commits to something you could disagree with.

## Rules
1. **One assertion per slide, in the headline.** The title is the claim, not the topic.
   "Personas" ✗ → "SMB merchants inflate prices twice as often as chains because they lack margin
   visibility" ✓.
2. **Design for the two-minute skim.** First pass = headlines + one visual per slide.
3. **Density through structure, not volume.** Under a slide cap, don't shrink text and add bullets;
   use a structure that carries more meaning per area (a 2×2 can replace eight bullets).
4. **One thing the eye lands on per slide.** All-text slides get skimmed and lost.
5. **Consistent and legible.** One type scale, one palette, aligned, real margins, enough contrast.
   A brief with a font floor is telling you this is checked.
6. **Consistent altitude.** Judges often flag mixing strategy-level slides with operational detail;
   decide the altitude and hold it.
7. **Executive summary first** when the brief asks, and whenever the deck exceeds ~6 slides.
8. Put the **prototype link** as a real hyperlink on an early slide, not only in the form.
